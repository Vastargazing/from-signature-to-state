# ICOs, IDOs, and Modern Crypto Fundraising

> **A token launch can raise capital, distribute network ownership, or provide exit liquidity. The label does not tell you which.**

## ICO

In an **Initial Coin Offering**, a project sells newly issued tokens directly, often before the network or product is mature.

Buyers fund development in exchange for an asset whose future utility and value may depend heavily on the team. The 2017-style public ICO made access broad but produced weak disclosure, scams, and major securities-law disputes.

## IDO

An **Initial DEX Offering** launches or sells a token through decentralized-exchange infrastructure or a launchpad, often creating an on-chain liquidity pool.

On-chain sale mechanics do not make the issuer, allocation, price discovery, or legal offering decentralized. Bots and insiders can dominate early blocks, and shallow liquidity can make the quoted valuation misleading.

## Fundraising today

Projects now mix several paths:

- founder equity and venture rounds;
- token warrants or future-token agreements;
- foundation grants and ecosystem funding;
- private token sales with vesting;
- public launchpads and auctions;
- airdrops, points, and user incentives;
- protocol revenue after launch.

The company, foundation, protocol, and token may have different rights and cash flows. Buying a token usually does not grant startup equity unless legal documents explicitly create that claim.

## Regulation is jurisdiction-specific

How a token is marketed, sold, and tied to managerial efforts can trigger offering, disclosure, consumer, tax, AML, and securities obligations. Rules and classifications change by country and over time.

Technical permissionlessness does not cancel issuer law.

## What to inspect

```text
who receives money?
what legal or protocol rights does the buyer get?
when do insider tokens unlock?
is the product live?
who controls minting and liquidity?
```

A fair-looking launch interface can sit on top of an extremely concentrated allocation.

## Check yourself

1. How does an IDO differ mechanically from a direct ICO?
2. Does a token normally represent equity in the development company?
3. Why does an on-chain sale not guarantee fair distribution?
4. Which allocation and control facts matter more than the launch label?
