# Market Capitalization, FDV, and Trading Volume

> **Market cap values circulating units at the latest price; FDV values a larger future supply; volume measures trading activity. None is cash stored in the asset.**

## Market capitalization

```text
market cap = current price × circulating supply
```

If 10 million tokens circulate at $2, market cap is $20 million.

This does not mean buyers deposited $20 million. The latest marginal trades set the quoted price, which is multiplied across all circulating units. Selling a large share can move that price sharply.

Circulating supply itself is a methodology: providers may disagree about locked treasury, bridged, vested, lost, or team-controlled tokens.

## Fully diluted valuation

```text
FDV = current price × maximum or projected total supply
```

FDV asks what valuation would result if all counted future units existed at today's price. It highlights dilution from unlocks and emissions.

It is not a forecast. Future supply can change demand, liquidity, yield, control, and price. Some tokens have no hard maximum, making “fully diluted” dependent on a chosen horizon or total-supply definition.

## Trading volume

Volume totals assets traded during a period, commonly 24 hours. High genuine volume can indicate liquidity and active price discovery.

But one unit can trade many times and count each time. Volume is not unique capital inflow. Wash trading, incentives, duplicated exchange reporting, and thin pools can distort it.

## Read the metrics together

A low-circulation token can show a modest market cap, huge FDV, and shallow liquidity. That combination warns that future unlocks may be large relative to today's tradable market.

Also inspect:

- circulating versus total supply;
- unlock schedule;
- order-book or pool depth;
- holder concentration;
- venue quality;
- real fees and usage.

One large headline number never describes market structure.
