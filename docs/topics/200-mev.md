# Maximal Extractable Value

> **MEV is value captured by choosing which transactions enter a block, in what order, or not at all—beyond ordinary block rewards and gas fees.**

## Ordering has economic power

Transactions do not arrive as one canonical queue. A block producer or builder chooses among valid pending transactions under the block gas limit.

If Alice's swap will move a DEX price, someone who sees it can place another trade before or after it. The same Ethereum state-transition rules execute, but the chosen order moves profit between participants.

MEV originally meant miner extractable value. **Maximal** is broader because validators, builders, sequencers, and other ordering actors can participate.

## Where the value comes from

Common opportunities include:

- arbitrage between inconsistent markets;
- liquidating an unsafe loan;
- placing trades around a user's swap;
- winning a scarce mint or auction;
- censoring or delaying a transaction;
- reorganizing recent history for an unusually large opportunity.

Some actions keep protocols healthy; others directly worsen a user's execution.

## The supply chain

Specialized searchers identify opportunities and construct ordered bundles. Builders combine bundles and public transactions into profitable blocks. Proposers choose a block to publish.

The captured value is divided through bids, tips, direct profit, and sometimes rebates to users.

## Why MEV cannot simply be deleted

Whenever transaction order changes outcomes, ordering rights have value. A protocol can hide pending transactions, use batch auctions, enforce fairer ordering, or return part of the value—but it must still choose an ordering rule.

MEV is therefore not one bug or one bot. It is the economic consequence of scarce block space plus state-dependent transactions.

The useful review question is: who can observe intent, who chooses order, and who receives the difference created by that order?
