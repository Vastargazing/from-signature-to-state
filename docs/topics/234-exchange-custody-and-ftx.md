# Keeping Funds on an Exchange: The FTX Lesson

> **A balance on a centralized exchange is the exchange's promise to deliver assets. It is not an on-chain balance controlled by your key.**

## What a deposit changes

When users send crypto to an exchange, the exchange controls the private keys and records each customer's claim in an internal database.

Trades move database balances. Users learn whether reserves are truly available only when withdrawals are processed—or fail.

## FTX

FTX collapsed in 2022 after customer assets were misappropriated and a large hole emerged between obligations and accessible assets. Customer funds had been routed into related-company activity rather than kept as a simple one-to-one pool ready for withdrawal.

The blockchain continued working. The failure was custody, accounting, governance, concentration, and fraud inside the intermediary.

## Proof of reserves is incomplete alone

Publishing wallet balances can show assets at a moment. It does not by itself show customer liabilities, pledged collateral, loans, legal ownership, hidden related parties, or whether keys can move funds after the snapshot.

A stronger picture needs assets, liabilities, controls, segregation, audits or attestations, and a clear insolvency regime.

## Why users still use exchanges

Centralized venues offer fiat rails, customer support, high-performance order books, recovery, and simple account management. Self-custody shifts responsibility to the user and is not automatically safer for everyone.

Reduce exposure by keeping only operational balances on venues, testing withdrawals, separating counterparties, enabling strong account security, and understanding jurisdiction and custody terms.

The key comparison is counterparty risk versus personal key-management risk—not convenience versus ideology.
