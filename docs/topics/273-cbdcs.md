# Central Bank Digital Currencies

> **A CBDC is digital central-bank money, not a decentralized cryptocurrency with a government logo.**

A central bank digital currency is denominated in a country's unit of account and is a direct liability of its central bank. That distinguishes it from a commercial-bank deposit, which is a liability of the bank, and from a stablecoin, which is a liability or arrangement created by a private issuer.

CBDCs do not require a public blockchain, mining, a token, or permissionless validators. A central bank may use a centralized ledger, distributed technology, or a hybrid architecture.

## Retail and wholesale

A retail CBDC is intended for households and businesses to use for everyday payments. A wholesale CBDC is intended for financial institutions and settlement between them, often alongside tokenized assets.

Many proposed retail designs are two-tiered: the central bank issues the money and maintains the core system, while banks or payment providers onboard users, operate wallets, perform compliance, and provide support.

## The design tradeoffs

A CBDC can provide direct digital central-bank money, payment competition, resilient public infrastructure, and programmable settlement between systems.

But the choices are political and operational as much as technical:

- Who can see transaction data?
- Can payments work offline?
- Are balances capped or interest-bearing?
- Can intermediaries freeze or recover funds?
- What happens during a network failure?
- Will people move deposits out of commercial banks during a crisis?

Privacy is not implied by “digital cash.” Every implementation must decide what users, intermediaries, the central bank, and law enforcement can observe.

## Do not confuse rails with money

A fast-payment app can move commercial-bank deposits without creating a CBDC. A blockchain can represent private stablecoins without creating central-bank money.

The decisive question is who owes the holder the money:

```text
CBDC → central-bank liability
bank deposit → commercial-bank liability
stablecoin → private issuer or protocol arrangement
```
