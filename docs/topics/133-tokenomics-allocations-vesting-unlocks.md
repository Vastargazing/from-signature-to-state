# Tokenomics: Allocations, Vesting, and Unlocks

> **Tokenomics is the map of who receives units, when they become transferable, what demand exists, and who can change the rules.**

## Allocation

A launch may divide supply among:

- community users and incentives;
- team and future employees;
- private investors;
- foundation or treasury;
- liquidity and market makers;
- validators, miners, or stakers.

Percentages alone are insufficient. Ten wallets may represent one fund, custodian, or founder. Look for beneficial control and voting power.

## Vesting and unlocks

A **cliff** blocks release until a date. **Linear vesting** then releases portions over time. Other schedules unlock by milestones, governance, or protocol emissions.

```text
allocation → locked or vested → unlocked → potentially circulating
```

Unlocked does not mean instantly sold. It means the holder gains the ability to transfer under the stated rules.

An on-chain vesting contract provides stronger enforcement than a spreadsheet or legal promise, but admins may retain cancellation, acceleration, or upgrade powers.

## Supply pressure is not the whole effect

Unlocks increase potential liquid supply and can change incentives. They may create sell pressure, governance concentration, staking growth, liquidity provision, or long-term participation.

Markets can anticipate known unlocks before the date. There is no mechanical formula saying a 10% unlock causes a 10% price fall.

## Demand and sinks

Good tokenomics analysis also asks why anyone needs the token:

- fees or collateral;
- governance;
- staking or security;
- redemption rights;
- access or utility;
- revenue claims, if legally and technically real.

Burns and lockups can reduce liquid supply, but circular rewards paid only by new issuance may hide weak organic demand.

## The control plane

Find mint authority, pausing, blacklisting, treasury keys, upgrade admins, governance quorum, and parameter bounds. A published cap is weak if privileged code can replace it.
