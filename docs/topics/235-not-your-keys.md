# Not Your Keys, Not Your Coins

> **If another party controls the signing keys, you own a claim governed by that party's systems and law—not unilateral blockchain control of the asset.**

## Control is the practical distinction

With self-custody, your valid signature can move funds under chain rules. An exchange, broker, lender, or custodian cannot normally block that transaction because it does not hold the key.

With custodial funds, the provider decides whether to honor withdrawal after account checks, outages, freezes, insolvency, or legal orders.

The slogan describes control, not necessarily legal ownership. A customer may still have a legal claim; the problem is that enforcing it can take time and may return less than the on-screen balance.

## Self-custody has sharp failure modes

Lost seed phrases, malicious signatures, compromised devices, fake addresses, inheritance failure, and incorrect backups can destroy access with no help desk.

Hardware wallets isolate keys but cannot tell whether a contract call is economically safe. Multisigs and recovery systems reduce one-key risk while adding more software and operational complexity.

## Keys are not coins

The key authorizes state changes. The asset remains recorded by the blockchain or token contract. Whoever can satisfy the account's authorization policy has effective control.

For smart wallets, that policy may be a threshold, recovery rule, or delegated code rather than one private key. The slogan generalizes to: know who can authorize your account.

## Choose consciously

Small spending balances, long-term savings, company treasuries, and active exchange collateral need different custody designs.

Test recovery before funding heavily. Separate devices and backups. Verify addresses and contract permissions. Do not move to self-custody merely to store one seed screenshot in cloud storage.

## Check yourself

1. What power does a custodian have that a self-custody provider does not?
2. Does the slogan decide legal ownership in bankruptcy?
3. Why does a hardware wallet not prevent malicious approvals?
4. How does the principle apply to a smart-contract wallet?
