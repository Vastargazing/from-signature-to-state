# ERC-721

> **ERC-721 represents individually identified tokens: ownership belongs to the pair of contract address and token ID.**

## Non-fungible identity

Within one ERC-721 contract, each token has a unique `uint256 tokenId` and at most one current owner.

```text
(chain ID, contract address, tokenId) → unique on-chain asset identity
```

Token ID `42` in another contract is unrelated. A copied name, image, or collection symbol creates no identity link.

Core operations include ownership lookup, transfer, per-token approval, and operator approval for all of an owner's tokens.

## Safe transfer

`safeTransferFrom` checks whether a contract recipient implements the ERC-721 receiver callback and returns the expected value. This prevents many NFTs from becoming stuck in contracts that cannot handle them.

Plain `transferFrom` skips that receiver check and can be unsafe when sending to contracts.

## Where the artwork lives

The NFT contract stores ownership. Metadata commonly comes from `tokenURI`, which may point to HTTPS, IPFS, Arweave, on-chain data, or a mutable server.

Owning the token does not automatically mean the image is permanent or that copyright transfers. Those properties depend on metadata design and legal terms.

An admin may be able to change base URIs, reveal metadata later, pause transfers, mint more tokens, or upgrade the contract.

## Approval risk

`approve` grants control of one token. `setApprovalForAll` grants an operator power over every ERC-721 owned now or acquired later under that contract.

Marketplace convenience therefore creates a broad authority similar to unlimited ERC-20 approval.

## Non-fungible does not mean unique content

Two tokens may point to the same image, and one image may be copied outside the chain. The unique object is the token record, not every media byte associated with it.
