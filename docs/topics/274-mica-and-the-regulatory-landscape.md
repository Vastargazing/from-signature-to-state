# MiCA and the Regulatory Landscape

> **MiCA gives much of the EU crypto market one rulebook, but it does not turn every token and protocol into the same legal object.**

The EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, or MiCA, covers crypto-assets and services not already governed by other EU financial-services law. Its stablecoin provisions applied from June 30, 2024, and the broader regime from December 30, 2024.

The maximum grandfathering period for existing crypto-asset service providers ended by July 1, 2026, although member states could choose a shorter period. Operating across the EU now generally requires the relevant MiCA authorization or another valid regulatory basis.

## What MiCA regulates

MiCA creates rules for several groups:

- issuers and offerors of many crypto-assets, including white-paper duties;
- issuers of asset-referenced tokens;
- issuers of e-money tokens;
- crypto-asset service providers such as trading, custody, exchange, and transfer businesses.

Authorized providers face governance, safeguarding, conduct, complaints, capital, and operational requirements. MiCA also introduces rules against crypto-asset market abuse.

## What “regulated” does not mean

A MiCA-compliant service is not risk-free, and authorization is not approval of every listed token. Smart-contract bugs, custody failures, price collapse, and fraud can still happen.

MiCA is also only one layer. AML rules, the EU transfer-of-funds regime, data protection, tax, sanctions, securities law, payments law, and national enforcement may apply beside it. A token classified as a financial instrument falls under other financial-market rules rather than simply MiCA.

Fully decentralized activity without an intermediary raises different scope questions from a company operating a frontend, custody service, or exchange. Labels such as “DAO,” “utility token,” or “NFT” do not decide the legal result by themselves; facts and functions do.

## The useful lens

```text
protocol rules decide what code permits
regulatory rules decide what people and businesses may offer
```

Both can apply to the same product, at different layers.

This chapter is an engineering map, not legal advice. Scope, authorization, transitional history, and enforcement can depend on the product and Member State.
