# “Ultrasound Money”: Thesis and Limits

> **The “ultrasound money” thesis combines low PoS issuance with fee burning, arguing that useful Ethereum demand can make ETH scarce. It is a thesis, not a guarantee.**

## The mechanism

After The Merge, Ethereum stopped issuing block rewards to PoW miners and continued lower issuance to PoS validators.

EIP-1559 burns execution base fees, while blob transactions add another burn stream through blob base fees.

```text
net ETH change = validator issuance - fee burns - other penalties
```

When burns exceed issuance, ETH supply decreases. When network demand is lower, issuance can exceed burns and supply grows.

The phrase “ultrasound money” markets this adaptive scarcity as stronger than a merely fixed schedule.

## What supports the thesis

ETH is required for protocol fees and staking. Activity creates base-fee burn, while staked capital secures consensus. If Ethereum and its rollups generate sustained demand for Ethereum data and settlement, the fee sink can be meaningful.

Proof of Stake also needs less issuance than a PoW system that must reimburse continuous external energy costs.

## The limitations

Net deflation is demand-dependent. Lower activity, cheaper data capacity, or changed fee markets can reduce burns.

Supply reduction does not guarantee price appreciation. Demand, liquidity, regulation, competition, risk, and market expectations also matter.

Very low issuance is not automatically optimal. Validator revenue must support decentralization and an adequate security budget. Excessively high user fees can push activity elsewhere.

Protocol governance can also change issuance and fee rules through future upgrades.

## Use the phrase precisely

Credible wording is:

```text
Ethereum has variable net issuance.
It can be deflationary when protocol burns exceed validator rewards.
```

Saying “ETH is permanently deflationary” is false. Saying “the supply only goes down” ignores observable periods of net issuance.
